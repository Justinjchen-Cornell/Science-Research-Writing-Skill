# Abstract Template
> Write LAST. Two models — pick one (journal usually decides):
> Model 1 = structured, components separate (broad topics) | Model 2 = flexible, components combined (narrow topics — MORE COMMON)
> Only fixed order rule: methodology before results.

## Model 1 — Structured

**① Background** [It is widely accepted that topic is important. A number of studies exist on X.]

**② Aim + Method** [The aim of this study was to investigate the use of [X] for [purpose]. An [instrument] was constructed/used to measure [Y].]

**③ Results** [It was found that [result]. The measurements were shown to lead to [achievement].]

**④ Implications** [This indicates that there is a possibility of [implication].]

## Model 2 — Flexible (combine components into 2-4 sentences)

**[S1 — paper + aim + method]** This study investigated the use of [X] as [purpose]. [Method summary: were synthesised/analysed/modelled using ...]

**[S2 — results with numbers]** It was found that [result with quantity], allowing for [achievement, e.g. a more consistent release rate of N% per hour].

**[S3 — implications, hedged]** These results suggest that [X] may provide [implication], although [limitation] are required to confirm these findings.

---
**Tense rules (ch06)**: Present Simple = gap/problem + what the paper does | Past Simple = what was done/found | may + verb = implication
**Word-limit survival**: every word must earn its place; adapt phrases from the article body, don't cut-and-paste whole sentences.
