# Cheatsheet — Act Like the Author

## Writing Order (the only workflow you need)
1. Methodology → Results (report sections, facts first)
2. Introduction (know what you did before describing it)
3. Discussion/Conclusion
4. Abstract (last — it derives from the article; easiest section)
5. Title (last)

## Section Models (one line each)
| Section | Components (in order) |
|---|---|
| Introduction | ① importance+background+define terms+problem area → ② literature review → ③ gap (However) → ④ present paper |
| Methodology | ① overview+purpose+source+background → ② details+justify+care → ③ relate to others → ④ problems |
| Results | ① revisit aim/method+overview → ② invite+key results+comparisons → ③ problems → ④ implications |
| Discussion | ① revisit previous sections+results → ② map to field → ③ achievements+implications → ④ limitations+future |
| Abstract | ① background/aim → ② methodology → ③ results/achievements → ④ applications → ⑤ (rare) limitations |

## Tense Cheatsheet
| Meaning | Tense |
|---|---|
| Accepted fact / what the paper does | Present Simple |
| Your completed actions | Past Simple |
| Research still relevant NOW / ongoing gap | Present Perfect |
| Standard procedure (passive) | Present Simple passive |
| What YOU did (passive) | Past Simple passive |
| Abstract: gap + paper description | Present Simple |

**Rule**: tense change = function change. Never change randomly.

## a / the in 10 seconds
- **the** → both you and reader know which one (shared context, unique referent: "the check-in", "the flame", "the centre")
- **a** → any one / reader doesn't know ("A 35 ml bottle was used")
- **a/an** by SOUND: an MRI, a UV light
- Singular countable nouns ALWAYS need a determiner

## Hedging ladder (use only as far as needed)
`x caused y` → `x was linked to y` → `The evidence suggests x caused y` → `x often caused y` → `x may have caused y`
**Too many softeners = empty sentence.** Never over-stack.

## Modal certainty scale
`can`(ability) < `may/might/could`(possible) < `should/ought to`(probable OR advisable) < `must/have to`(virtually certain, no proof)
**must ≠ has to in negative**: must not = forbidden; doesn't have to = not necessary. **cannot** = impossible (≠ can not = possibly not).

## Frequency scale (11 anchors)
always/invariably → generally/usually → regularly → often → more often than not → **as often as not (50%)** → sometimes → occasionally → rarely → hardly ever → never

## Quantity framing
Big: considerable, substantial, as many as 45, over half — Small: barely, negligible, as few as 45, few — Emphasise: even higher, by far, exceptionally — Similar: almost, nearly — Uncommitted: some, moderate, fairly, to some extent

## Signal words by function (quick pick)
Cause: due to/because/since — Result: therefore/as a result/hence — Contrast: however/whereas/while — Unexpected: although/despite/nevertheless — Addition: moreover/furthermore/in addition

## Latin plurals (90% of cases)
-um→-a (datum→data, medium→media) · -us→-i (nucleus→nuclei, radius→radii) · -is→-es (analysis→analyses, hypothesis→hypotheses) · -on→-a (criterion→criteria, phenomenon→phenomena) · -ix→-ices (matrix→matrices, index→indices) · -a→-ae (formula→formulae)
**data = plural** → "the data show" (never "shows")

## Anti-patterns to check before submitting
- [ ] Every sentence connected (overlap/pro-form/connector)?
- [ ] One-sentence paragraphs or walls of text?
- [ ] Own work distinguishable from standard procedure (tense split)?
- [ ] Every claim hedged exactly as much as the evidence allows?
- [ ] Figures commented on ("as many as 23%" not just "23%")?
- [ ] Limitation paired with a positive + future direction?
- [ ] Novelty claims hedged "to our knowledge"?
- [ ] Title: sentence case, specific, no modals, no "A study of…"?
- [ ] Abstract self-contained (survives alone in a database)?
- [ ] Native-speaker check of final text?
