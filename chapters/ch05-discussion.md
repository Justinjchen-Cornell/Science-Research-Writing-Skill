# Chapter 5: Unit 4 — Writing the Discussion/Conclusion

## Core Idea
The Discussion walks the Introduction **in reverse**: it looks back at the map you drew (existing research), shows where your work fits on it, states what your achievements mean, and ends by opening the door for future work. It is where you move the reader *out* of the article.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The 4-Component Discussion Model**:
  1. **Revisiting previous sections**: revisit previous research → recall weaknesses in their methodology → revisit your methodology → summarise/revisit general or key results
  2. **Mapping**: show where/how the present work fits into the research map of the field
  3. **Achievement/contribution**: recall a positive aspect of the results → focus on its meaning and implications → note novelty ("to our knowledge…") → refine implications, including applications
  4. **Limitations** (that direct future work) → **current and future work** → applications
- **Modal Verbs as Truth-Value Control** (the Discussion's core grammar):
  - No modal = empirical claim ("The drop was due to a crack in the pipe")
  - **may/might/could/can** = possible/optional
  - **should/ought to** = probable/likely OR advisable (two meanings — know which you mean!)
  - **must/have to** = virtually certain (but still no proof — "It must be ten o'clock" is said when you DON'T know) OR necessary
  - **can** = able/capable; **could** = was generally capable in the past; **was able to** = specific past occasion (safer choice)
  - Negatives are traps: *must not* ≠ *doesn't have to*; *cannot* = impossible (≠ can not = possibly not); may not/might not ≠ could not/cannot
  - Past forms: may have / might have / could have (NOT "can have")
- **Novelty claims**: check thoroughly (not just the Internet) before "the first study to…"; always hedge with "to our knowledge"

## Key Concepts
- **Limitations for direction, not confession**: mention limitations that CAN be addressed in future work — it's an invitation to the research community, and it makes studies that follow yours cite you
- **Positive-outcome pairing**: mention a positive result next to every limitation ("Although our hypotheses were supported statistically, the sample was not reassessed…")
- **therefore** links limitation → future research
- **No obvious application?** Check your Introduction's first sentences (they show why the area is useful) and Discussions of related work; some research legitimately clarifies theory — don't invent applications

## Mental Models
- "Being certain ≠ knowing" — modal verbs signal the absence of proof, not its presence
- "The best studies open up directions for research" — a specific future-work proposal is more likely to be carried out, and follows-on research cites you

## Anti-patterns
- **"must" used to sound stronger** — it actually communicates lack of evidence; use must only for near-certainty without proof, or necessity
- **Forgetting the reverse-order symmetry**: Discussion must re-enter the Introduction's map, not start from scratch
- **Over-hedging**: stack too many softeners ("The evidence points to the possibility that in many cases x can contribute to…") and the sentence means nothing

## Worked Example
Model Discussion (CBSM stress management study) maps to the components:
- S1-S2 → Component 1: revisit previous research + its weaknesses ("However, these studies have either been short-term…")
- S3-S4 → Component 1: your methodology + results summary ("We found that in virtually all cases…")
- S5-S6 → Component 2: mapping ("These findings extend those of Kaliom…") + achievement ("unrelated to age, gender or ethnic background")
- S7-S9 → Component 3: implications ("may address QoL needs across a wide range of patients"; "Most notably, this is the first study to our knowledge…")
- S10-S12 → Component 4: limitations → future work ("Future work should therefore include follow-up work…")

## Key Takeaways
1. Start by revisiting previous research and your own results; end with future work
2. Use the modal system to calibrate every claim's certainty
3. Mention limitations only as doors for future research, paired with positives
4. Claim novelty only with "to our knowledge" after real checking
5. Specify future directions — vague suggestions get no follow-up and no citations
