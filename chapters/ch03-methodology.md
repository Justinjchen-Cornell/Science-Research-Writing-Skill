# Chapter 3: Unit 2 — Writing about Methodology

## Core Idea
The Methodology must let readers **replicate** your work — but it's more than a log: it must also justify choices, show care was taken, relate to other studies, and (honestly) flag problems. Unlike the Introduction model, this is a **menu**: select only the components that apply to your work.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The 4-Component Methodology Model (menu)**:
  1. **General intro/overview** → restate the purpose → give source of materials/equipment → supply essential background information
  2. **Specific & precise details** (quantities, temperatures, duration, sequence, conditions, locations, sizes) → **justify choices made** → **indicate appropriate care was taken**
  3. **Relate materials/methods to other studies** (cite the method's origin)
  4. **Indicate where problems occurred** (only if you had any — built equipment? skip component 1's source item)
- **Passive Voice & the Standard-vs-Your-Work Tense Split**:
  - Present Simple passive ("a flexible section *is* inserted") = standard procedure / how equipment is normally built
  - Past Simple passive ("the samples *were* collected") = what you did yourself
  - **The single most dangerous error**: mixing these up makes your own contribution invisible — the reader can't separate standard procedure from your innovation (e.g. "The pulses *were* split…" marks YOUR key step by a tense change)
  - Agentless passives are the norm; mark your work with "In this study / here / in our model" or a dummy subject ("This experiment…")
- **a vs the rules**:
  - THE if you and your reader both know which thing (shared knowledge, only-one-possible-referent: "the check-in was closed", "the flame went out")
  - A if it doesn't matter / you don't know / reader doesn't know ("A 35 ml bottle was used")
  - a/an follows SOUND not spelling: an MRI scan, a UV light
  - Singular countable nouns need a determiner (a/the/my/this/one/some)
- **Adverb safety**: avoid ambiguous adverb clusters ("The lecture on cancer in January" — in January = lecture or cancer?); front the sentence with sentence-level adverbs; split ambiguous sentences into units

## Key Concepts
- **Guide for Authors**: check the target journal's guide first — it states whether to write active or passive, and the section's expected title (Materials and Methods / Procedure / Experimental / Simulation / Model)
- **Citing another researcher's identical procedure**: same Past Simple passive — you MUST separate with "In this study…" vs "In Brown (1999)…" or references, or the reader can't tell whose work it is
- **Five uses of passive tenses** (what was done by me / by the cited researcher / standard procedure / as you can see in the figure / field-required Present Simple)

## Mental Models
- "You are now writing for people who learn FROM you, not teachers who check you" — the reader must be able to carry out and accept your procedure
- "Make your contribution obvious to the reader — it is obvious to YOU, that's the trap"
- "Minimise problems, don't hide them": vocabulary that minimises the problem and your responsibility while suggesting a solution ("variation among samples was negligible")

## Anti-patterns
- **Confusing standard procedure with your own work** (the tense error with serious consequences)
- **Uncountable-noun mistakes**: death, childhood, industry, steel CAN be countable ("three deaths", "many industries")
- **Invisible errors**: adverb location and a/the errors are invisible to proofreaders — grammatically correct but wrong meaning
- **"by us / by me" in formal passives**: omit the agent

## Worked Example
Model Methodology (groundwater chemistry, London Basin) maps to the menu:
- S1-S2 → Component 1: overview + background + justification ("sites were selected from the London Basin area, which…")
- S3-S5 → Component 2: precise details + care ("thoroughly-rinsed 25 ml brown glass bottles… filled to the top and then sealed tightly to prevent contamination")
- S6-S8 → Component 3: related to existing method ("a revised version of the precipitation method established by the ISF Institute in Germany")
- S9 → Component 4: problem minimised ("two samples were at risk of CFC contamination… variation among samples was negligible")

## Key Takeaways
1. Follow the Guide for Authors: active vs passive is usually dictated
2. Use Present Simple passive for standards, Past Simple passive for your own steps
3. Mark your contribution with "in this study / here" whenever the same passive could describe someone else's work
4. Give quantities, durations, temperatures, sequence — precision is the point
5. Acknowledge problems with minimising language, paired with a positive
