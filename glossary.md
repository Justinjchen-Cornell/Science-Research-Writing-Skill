# Glossary

**Agentless passive** — a passive sentence with no agent ("The samples were collected"), the norm in formal science writing (Ch 3)

**As often as not** — the neutral frequency point (50%): occurred as often as it didn't (Ch 4)

**Compound noun** — nouns stacked with the right-hand noun as head ("an oil can opener repair man training programme" = ambiguous); keep compounds short and unambiguous (Ch 6)

**Conventional model** — the familiar, expected structure readers can parse instantly; language errors matter less inside it (Ch 1)

**Dummy subject** — "This paper… / The present paper…" used instead of "we" or agentless passive to announce the paper itself (Ch 2)

**Frequency language** — the 11-group scale from always/invariably (100%) to never (0%) used to signal result reliability (Ch 4)

**Gap in the research** — the space your paper fills; introduced with However/Although, stated as prediction/hypothesis rather than a question (Ch 2)

**Guide for Authors** — the journal's published instructions on section titles, active/passive choice, and Abstract model; consult before writing (Ch 3)

**Literature review ordering** — chronological / by approach-theory / general-to-specific; avoids the "tennis match" of alternating However…On the other hand (Ch 2)

**Mapping (Discussion)** — locating your study on the field's research map drawn in the Introduction (Ch 5)

**Meeting-place fact** — the most general background fact everyone shares; the safe opening for an Introduction (Ch 2)

**Modal verbs** — may/might/could/can/should/ought to/need to/have to/must; modify the truth value of a claim (Ch 5)

**Pro-form** — a pronoun or demonstrative (it, this method, these systems) that glues sentences together (Ch 2)

**Quantity language** — the 5-group system that frames numbers as big/small/emphasised/similar/uncommitted (Ch 4)

**Sequence language** — the 8-group system for precise timing: beforehand → at the beginning → then → soon → later → when/simultaneously → at the end → afterwards (Ch 4)

**Signalling language** — sentence connectors grouped by function (cause/result/contrast/unexpectedness/addition) (Ch 2)

**Target articles** — 3-4 real research papers from your field used as templates; from English-speaking institutions, <15 A4 pages, IMRD-subtitled (Ch 1)

**Tense pairs** — Present Simple (facts) vs Continuous (temporary); Past Simple (your study) vs Present Perfect (still-relevant research) (Ch 2)

**The Three Questions** — for every section: how to start, what info in what order, how to end (Ch 1)

**To our knowledge** — the mandatory hedge on novelty claims ("the first study to our knowledge…") (Ch 5)
