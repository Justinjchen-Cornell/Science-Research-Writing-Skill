# Demo: From Broken Draft to Publication-Ready Abstract

> The fastest way to see what this skill does. We take a typical non-native
> speaker's raw draft, then rewrite it **only** using the models and language
> systems from this skill (Abstract model, tense rules, hedging ladder,
> signalling language). Every rewrite decision is annotated.

---

## ❌ The Draft (what the author wrote)

> We studied nanoparticles for drug delivery. We prepared gold nanoparticles
> and tested them. The results were very good. Nanoparticles can kill cancer
> cells. This research is very important.

**What's wrong (diagnosed with the skill's own tools):**

| Problem | Diagnostic (skill chapter) |
|---|---|
| No structure — no aim, no method, no result framing | ch06: Abstract model missing all components |
| Tense chaos: "can kill" implies an established truth, but it's your finding | ch02: tense pairs — findings need Past Simple |
| "We studied / We prepared" — no passive, no method detail | ch03: agentless passive + precision |
| "Very good / very important" — claims with zero hedging or evidence | ch04: quantity language + ch05: modal calibration |
| No linking between sentences | ch02: sentence connection |

---

## ✅ The Rewrite (Abstract Model 2 — flexible, results-focused)

> **This study investigated the use of gold nanoparticles as a drug delivery
> platform for cancer therapy.** Nanoparticles were synthesised by citrate
> reduction and characterised using transmission electron microscopy.
> It was found that the nanoparticles were taken up by HeLa cells within
> 2 hours, and that drug release was sustained over a 72-hour period.
> **The results suggest that gold nanoparticles may provide a viable
> platform for targeted drug delivery, although in vivo studies are required
> to confirm these findings.**

---

## 🔍 Every Decision Annotated

| Sentence | Component (ch06 model) | Language decisions |
|---|---|---|
| *This study investigated...* | ① What the paper does + aim + method, combined in one sentence (Model 2 style) | "This study" = dummy subject, not "we" (ch02) |
| *Nanoparticles were synthesised by citrate reduction and characterised...* | ② Methodology | Agentless passive, Past Simple = what was done in this study (ch03) |
| *It was found that... were taken up... was sustained...* | ③ Results | "It was found that" = results framing; Past Simple keeps findings tied to this study, not claimed as universal truths (ch02) |
| *The results suggest that gold nanoparticles may provide...* | ③→④ Implications | Hedging ladder (ch04): "suggest that" (weaker than "show") + "may provide" (modal = possible, ch05) |
| *...although in vivo studies are required to confirm these findings.* | ⑤ Limitations/Future work (brief) | "although" = signalling language for unexpectedness/contrast (ch02); turns the limitation into a future direction (ch05) |

---

## 💡 What Just Happened

- **Model provided the skeleton** — every sentence now has a known function (aim → method → results → implications → limitation)
- **Tense system provided the meaning** — Present Simple "This study investigates" vs Past Simple "were synthesised" vs "may provide": three time/truth levels, one paragraph
- **Hedging ladder provided the honesty** — "suggest" + "may" + "are required to confirm" = claims calibrated exactly to the evidence
- **Zero original research content was added** — same facts, professional form. This is the entire point: *science writing is conventional; the skill makes the convention executable*
